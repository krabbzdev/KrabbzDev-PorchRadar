Settings

Three steps and you're watching the sky:
  1. Tell it where home is. Type your ZIP code or address below, pick it from the list, then press Save. You only do this once.
  2. Click the page once for sound. Browsers keep a page silent until you click it. After that, a plane overhead gets a chime and a spoken line.
  3. Watch the map. Planes inside your blue circle are listed on the left. One passing right overhead opens a full-screen close-up.

⭕ Overhead distance

How close a plane has to pass to set off the overhead alerts: the full-screen close-up and the voice. It's the red dashed circle around home on the map, and changes as you drag.

🌙 Quiet hours

to

The close-up and the pop-ups still appear. Times use this device's clock.

👀 Watch list

Get a pop-up and a spoken heads-up when a plane you care about is anywhere in range. Type words separated by commas: an aircraft type or code (747, B52, blimp), an owner or airline (Coast Guard, NASA), a flight or tail number, or the words military or helicopter.

🚨 Emergency bar distance

The red bar at the top of the page shows planes with an emergency (squawk 7500, 7600 or 7700, or an emergency status) that are within this distance of home. Planes farther away don't set it off.

🗣 Announcement voice

A voice speaks when a plane passes inside the overhead circle.

Anything the app doesn't know for a plane (like where it's going) is left out.

Kokoro voices sound the most natural and are made right on this PC. Heart is the default: it downloads by itself the first time the app opens (about 90 to 300 MB, once) and the browser keeps it. The browser's own voices are instant; Edge's "Natural" ones only work when Microsoft's speech service can be reached. Each browser remembers its own choice.

To turn the alerts on or off, use Overhead Plane Alert and 🔔 Sound at the top of the page.

🔍 Size of everything

Make text, buttons and panels bigger or smaller. Handy on a TV or wall screen. Changes right away.

🎨 Theme

The colours of the dashboard. Changes right away. Daylight also switches the map to the Light style (and the others back to Dark) when the map is on one of those two; other map styles stay as you set them.

📊 Stat cards

📏 Units

Off: miles per hour and miles. Altitudes are always in feet. The voice uses the same units.

Turn these on or off in 🗺 Map Options on the main page. Here you set how they look.

📡 Radar sweep strength

How strong the beam looks when 📡 Radar sweep is on (Map Options). Lower is fainter. Changes right away.

〰 Line strength

How strong the lines behind planes look on the map: the 〰 Flight history lines and the ↝ Plane tails (both in Map Options). Lower is fainter. Changes right away.

〰 Flight history length

How far back the 〰 Flight history lines go (Map Options). Shorter keeps the map cleaner. Changes right away. The app only remembers the last 3 hours, and starts counting again whenever it restarts.

🚢 Ship tracking optional, free

Shows cruise ships and other boats near you, live, using AISStream.io. It's free, but you need your own key:

  1. Go to aisstream.io and click Sign in (with your GitHub account).
  2. Open API Keys and click Create.
  3. Copy the key and paste it below, then click Save.

Your key stays on this PC (in settings.json). AISStream receives the area around your home so it can send the ships there.

The app remembers where these lines' ships were last seen anywhere in the world, so you can jump to them by name. AISStream can watch up to 190 ships, so choosing fewer lines leaves room for all of their ships.

✈️ Flight plans optional, your own FlightAware key

Fills in where each flight really is going (and its departure and arrival times) on the plane card. It uses FlightAware's AeroAPI. That is a separate product from a FlightAware website membership: create a key at flightaware.com/aeroapi, then paste it below.

Need a key for these advanced features? Sign up for a personal AeroAPI account here.

AeroAPI charges per request, so the app asks about a flight only when you open its card, at most once every 10 minutes. The key stays on this PC (in settings.json).

🛰 Backup plane feed optional, your own OpenSky account

If the main plane feed (adsb.fi) is down, the app can ask the OpenSky Network for the planes near you instead. It does this only while the main feed is failing, and at most every 30 seconds. Create an API client on your OpenSky account page and paste its client ID and secret here. OpenSky does not send aircraft type or tail number.

When it is used, your home area (a box around your Tracker Radius) is sent to OpenSky. The client ID and secret stay on this PC (in settings.json).

📋 FAA owner records optional

The full FAA record for US planes (co-owners, city and state, serial number, registration dates). The app downloads the registry for you (a small file from GitHub, or the FAA's own 73 MB file) once and keeps it in its own folder. It never updates itself: press the button again whenever you want newer records.

📡 FlightAware feeder optional

Only if you run your own ADS-B receiver that feeds FlightAware. Your stats then show in the right-hand panel.

Advanced: photo lookup contact

Planespotters.net asks each app for a contact email or web page. Leave the default unless you have a reason to change it.

🌐 Network

Off: only this PC can open it. On: phones, tablets and TVs on the same Wi-Fi can open it in their browser too. They can watch, but only this PC can change settings, favorites or updates. The first time, Windows may show a firewall popup asking to allow Node.js JavaScript Runtime (the app is built on Node.js): tick Private networks and click Allow access. If you missed it, other devices can't connect until you allow it in Windows Security → Firewall & network protection → Allow an app through firewall. Only turn this on at home, not on public Wi-Fi.
